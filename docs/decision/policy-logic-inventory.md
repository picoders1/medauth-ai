# Policy Logic Inventory

What shape each policy version's decision rule has, and what about it is still
unknown. Generated by `scripts/build_logic_inventory.py` into
`data/policy_logic/inventory.json`; this is the reading of it.

**One in eight policy versions has a declared logic. Four of eight carry wording
that a conjunction may misread and have no declaration.**

## What the four forms mean

**`DECLARED`** — the logic was read off the regulation and written down. Only
42 CFR 410.32 qualifies, and only for its exception; two other questions about that
policy remain open (below).

**`REVIEW_REQUIRED`** — the version carries exception, alternative, conditional or
exclusion wording, and nobody has established what its logic actually is. This is
the honest classification for four versions, and it is **not** the same as saying
their logic is wrong.

**`ASSUMED_CONJUNCTION`** — no signal was found. That is weak evidence, not a
finding: the search is lexical and cannot read meaning, and the 9 provisions per
version still awaiting review have not been read by anyone qualified.

**`NO_CRITERIA`** — 42 CFR 410.43 is in the corpus with nothing transcribed from
it, so there is no logic to state. Its 21 provisions sit in the OD-19 queue.

## The gap you should know about

**The inventory records a doubt the runtime does not act on.**

Four versions are marked `REVIEW_REQUIRED` here. At runtime they still receive an
`ASSUMED_CONJUNCTION`, because no declared-logic file exists for them and the
engine's default is what it always was.

Closing that gap means one of two things, and neither is free:

1. **Declare their logic** — which requires reading their provisions, i.e. OD-19.
2. **Route them to `HUMAN_REVIEW`** — correct in principle, and it would invalidate
   every gold_v1 case for 410.33, 410.38 and 411.15, forcing gold_v2 on the basis
   of a lexical signal scan.

Neither is done. Picking the convenient one would be worse than recording the gap,
so it is recorded as **OD-24**.

## Signals are search terms, not classifications

The scanner looks for wording — "except", "unless", "either", "only if", "not
covered", "at least" — and it cannot tell an exception from a cross-reference. Every
signal is a place for a reviewer to look. None is a finding.

That caution is the whole lesson of the 410.32 defect. The engine assumed a shape
and was wrong; a scanner that assumes a different shape would be no better.

## Unresolved semantics, by policy

### 42 CFR 410.32 — declared, and still not settled

The mammography exception at (a)(1) is declared and tested. Three things are not:

- **C03 depends on untranscribed text.** "The appropriate level of supervision"
  refers to the levels set out in paragraph (b)(3), which is not transcribed. The
  criterion cannot be adjudicated on its own evidence (**R-51**). It is declared as
  a plain conjunct because that is what the engine already did — whether it should
  instead be conditional on the test's designated level is a question for review.
- **C04 over-applies.** Portable x-ray general supervision applies only to portable
  x-ray services in a place of residence. That is conditional applicability, which
  `When` can express, but no transcribed criterion establishes whether a case *is*
  a portable x-ray service. Declared as a plain conjunct, which over-applies it.
- **The exception's scope is read narrowly.** (a)(1) relieves the treating-physician
  requirement on its face. Whether it also relieves C02 (results used in management)
  is not stated. The narrow reading is conservative for approval and aggressive for
  denial, so it is flagged rather than settled.

### 42 CFR 410.33 — every signal type, no declaration

Five signal types across 38 provisions awaiting review, including a numeric limit
(a supervising physician may not cover more than three sites) already transcribed
as an EXCLUSION. Whether the rest of the policy is conjunctive is unknown.

### 42 CFR 410.38 — both revisions

Exception and conditional wording in both. The known gap is `(d)(1)(ii)(A)`, the
prior-to-delivery timing condition identified in Phase 3 and ranked priority 1 in
the OD-19 package: criteria C02 and C03 both plausibly depend on it.

### 42 CFR 411.15 — 100 provisions awaiting review

The largest queue in the corpus, and an exclusion overlay: it states what is *not*
covered and has nothing to satisfy. A policy with no requirements cannot approve —
vacuous truth would turn it into a universal approval, which is refused explicitly
and tested.

### 42 CFR 410.61 — the only versions with no signal

Both revisions scan clean. The plan-of-treatment requirements do read as a
conjunction. This is the weakest kind of evidence, and 9 provisions per version are
still unread.
